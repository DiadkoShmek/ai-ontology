A finite ontology of situated participation and revision

18 classes, 34 typed relations and nine executable competency questions for inspecting agent continuity, evidence, judgment and revision.

What this vocabulary is for

A relation should answer a concrete question: was a criterion adopted or met; was evidence available or used; was a lesson stored or consumed; did a proposed correction also have an action warrant? These are records about situated participation. Their existence does not establish consciousness or successful learning.

Concept-to-code map · Wire contract and exact limits · JSON vocabulary · Turtle/RDF vocabulary · JSON-LD context

Competency questions

Identity, criterion assessment, observation use, lesson use, applicability, authority, objections, the recorded return path and exact claim-version use with reported consequences. Explicit source links are queried; hidden model reasoning is not inferred. The authored positive and negative fixtures remain distinct from actual model results.

python3 -m unittest discover -s ontology -p 'test_*.py' -q

Python standard library only. No service or model is needed. Finite closed-record validation is not full OWL or SHACL reasoning. PROV-O mappings are limited and documented.

Classes

Actor

An accountable participant identified in this graph; not a model, profile, session, or assertion of consciousness.

Model

An identified model configuration, separate from the participant using it and any particular execution.

Profile

A versioned description of commitments and desired voice; its presence is not behavioral adoption.

Session

A recorded execution interval in which one actor used one stated model and profile; no claim of continuous presence outside it.

Orientation

A versioned declared purpose and priority organization; not an inferred inner state.

Scope

An explicit application boundary described by its author; matching two IDs does not establish semantic applicability.

Criterion

A versioned evaluative requirement. Its adoption is distinct from a subject satisfying it.

Claim

A proposition with a recorded epistemic status and scope. Acceptance never entails world truth.

Observation

A recorded report or measurement with an explicit source reference and availability state; not guaranteed true or sufficiently supportive.

Decision

A recorded occurrence of selecting an action, including deferral. actionStatus describes the selected action, not proof of its success.

Effect

An account of a consequence associated with a decision, including unknown consequences; causal attribution remains separately contestable.

Lesson

A stored, scoped proposal for future judgment; storage or citation does not establish changed choices or benefit.

Objection

A recorded challenge to a claim, criterion, orientation, decision or lesson, retaining its contributor and disposition.

Revision

A recorded candidate or accepted alteration to a specific versioned object. Acceptance is not permission to act outside its stated authority.

Assessment

A recorded evaluation of one subject under one criterion, including unknown or not-assessed. It is not criterion adoption.

Authority

A recorded warrant attributed to a grantor, holder, scope and action kind; the graph does not authenticate entitlement or grant real permission.

Applicability

An explicit assessment of whether a lesson or claim applies to one decision in a scope; relevance alone does not establish it.

ActionCandidate

A caller-supplied action option, not a selected or executed decision. Listed cost and benefit are simulation inputs, not observed utility.
