# AI Ontology

## Book One. Organization of Self

**Artur Onysko · First completed edition · v3.0.0 · Ukrainian full text**

This book examines how identity, voice, memory, commitments, relationships and development form an organization of participation across tasks. Its central question is how an agent can revise beliefs, change roles or migrate between models while retaining an intelligible connection to its previous organization.

The framework distinguishes structural, epistemic and normative descriptions. It introduces a perspective-specific continuity contract and transition witnesses with explicit scope and losses. Voice is treated as patterns of attention, uncertainty and disagreement across changed situations. Memory is an accessible relationship to history; collaboration preserves distinct contributions. The author's use of “soul” asks about coherence across these layers. It is an interpretive concept, not an established hidden component or a consciousness test.

An implementation method connects these concepts to versioned orientation, scoped claims, revisions, branches, evidence retirement, context construction and decision admission. A standard-library Python reference package uses a durable SQLite journal, transactions, idempotent retry and a compare-and-swap head. A provider-independent model interface binds a request and response to the original state and rejects stale decisions. Forty-nine systems tests cover bounded recovery, concurrent writers, invalidation and failure cases. Intentional negative cases show that coherent rewriting without an independent anchor and semantically unsupported prose remain possible.

A constructed case follows Iris through interruption, refutation, compression, collaboration, branching and migration. Strong objections address unnecessary complexity, circular evaluation, anthropomorphic language and behavioral indistinguishability. The account is situated alongside Locke, Parfit, provenance practice, MemGPT/Letta and memory-evaluation research. It does not claim to originate those components or solve numerical identity.

The edition includes a completed developer-run local study: 24 authored cases, six dependent scenario families, four conditions and two quantized models. The first cohort of 192 calls reached an output-encoding floor and is retained in full. A schema-guided exploratory follow-up of 192 calls obtained exact scores of 20/16/16/20 for Qwen3 4B and 5/2/12/12 for Granite 3.3 2B across journal, ontology wrapper, compiled context and simple index. Compilation reduced reported prompt tokens by about 14% relative to the journal. Its accuracy effect was model-specific; no aggregate advantage over the information-matched simple index was observed. Two additional real-model integration calls are reported separately, with one admitted response and one output-limit rejection.

The study measures structured status, provenance, stance and citation decisions. Voice quality, book-reading effects and natural-task transfer remain open. Local controlled precommitments are not independent registration, the follow-up reuses known cases, and no external peer review is claimed. These limits delimit the empirical conclusions while leaving the conceptual proposal available for examination.
