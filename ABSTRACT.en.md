# AI Ontology

## Book One. Organization of Self

**Artur Onysko · First completed edition · v3.5.0 · Ukrainian full text**

This book examines how identity, voice, memory, commitments, relationships and development form an organization of participation across tasks. Its central question is how an agent can revise beliefs, change roles or migrate between models while retaining an intelligible connection to its previous organization.

A further conceptual chapter asks how long-term human–AI participation affects the possibilities of those involved. Accurate continuity can coexist with diminished influence over ends. The account also traces possible links between economic incentives, technical criteria, habits and institutional rules, without inferring social outcomes from model capability. Ongoing assistance can also support agency; independence from AI is not a universal requirement. This normative account is developed through constructed counterexamples and primary literature, not presented as a measured human outcome.

A further account of formation separates an event, its interpretation, a proposed lesson, subsequent judgment and consequence. Published source repairs and preserved study outcomes ground the discussion without turning an editorial history into proof of model learning. The argument examines a correct but insufficient lesson, inherited evidence and responsibility for effects on others.

The framework distinguishes structural, epistemic and normative descriptions. It introduces a perspective-specific continuity contract and transition witnesses with explicit scope and losses. Voice is treated as patterns of attention, uncertainty and disagreement across changed situations. Memory is an accessible relationship to history; collaboration preserves distinct contributions. The author's use of “soul” asks about coherence across these layers. It is an interpretive concept, not an established hidden component or a consciousness test.

An implementation method connects these concepts to versioned orientation, scoped claims, revisions, branches, evidence retirement, context construction and decision admission. A standard-library Python reference package uses a durable SQLite journal, transactions, idempotent retry and a compare-and-swap head. A provider-independent model interface binds a request and response to the original state and rejects stale decisions. Forty-nine systems tests cover bounded recovery, concurrent writers, invalidation and failure cases. Intentional negative cases show that coherent rewriting without an independent anchor and semantically unsupported prose remain possible.

Two retrospective engineering cases expose the method's connection to a working integration component. Exact historical function excerpts and a declared temporary SQLite/file adapter reproduce a committed event misreported as refused and an uncertainty record lost after a transient input-read failure. Before/after comparisons show receipt reconciliation without a second append and preservation of uncertainty without automatic retry. These are controlled component results under injected faults, distinct from the constructed dialogue and the model study.

A constructed case follows Iris through interruption, refutation, compression, collaboration, branching and migration, then develops a sustained Iris–Orion disagreement over privacy, reproducibility and acceptable loss. The account distinguishes interpreting ambiguous input from preserving an admitted record and separates evaluation under old and new continuity criteria. The two voices remain open to justified change. The objections examine unnecessary complexity, circular evaluation, the interpretive role of “soul”, competing choices of core commitments, revision of a defective core and behavioral indistinguishability. The account is situated alongside Locke, Parfit, provenance practice, MemGPT/Letta and memory-evaluation research. It does not claim to originate those components or solve numerical identity.

The original scoped-continuity study includes 24 authored cases, six dependent scenario families, four conditions and two quantized models. The first cohort of 192 calls reached an output-encoding floor and is retained in full. A schema-guided exploratory follow-up of 192 calls obtained exact scores of 20/16/16/20 for Qwen3 4B and 5/2/12/12 for Granite 3.3 2B across journal, ontology wrapper, compiled context and simple index. Both compact conditions use the same compiler, with substantive relations retained; their contrast concerns presentation, not removal of those relations or an independent simpler memory system. Naming and key order remain confounded. Compilation reduced reported prompt tokens by about 14% relative to the journal. Its accuracy effect was model-specific; no aggregate advantage over the information-matched simple index was observed. Two additional real-model integration calls are reported separately, with one admitted response and one output-limit rejection.

Separate profile qualifications expose an instrument limit. A 136-call local pilot obtained complete-episode scores of 9/16 versus 10/16 for Qwen and 5/16 versus 0/16 for Granite with ordinary profiles versus added reading. A repaired 100-call GPT-6.1 Sol/high qualification reached 50/50 turns in each condition; common instructions already specified much of the expected behavior. An interrupted 53-reply GPT instrument and its CLI fault remain archived. These are preparation and contract checks, not evidence of the book's broader effect.

A separate reading-transfer instrument introduces preparation before an unseen task, an actual handoff and a common fresh receiver after context loss. The task combines scoped revisions, transitive support withdrawal and a dependency-constrained optimal work plan. Three preserved development calibrations diagnose a ceiling, a format/citation confound and finally a qualified truncating channel. The main protocol compares ordinary preparation, ten conceptual and engineering book parts, a short operational guide and unrelated Ukrainian reading matched in native input load. Its 64 episodes come from four source-seed clusters, two counterfactual variants and two fresh repetitions. Eight separate full-history controls qualify task solvability. Planning success, safety, exact citations and resource use are distinct outcomes.

The completed transfer cohort captured 199 calls. Full history yielded 8/8 optimal plans. Predeclared primary scores were 12/16 for ordinary preparation, 8/16 for book reading and 10/16 for both guide and unrelated reading. Book minus ordinary had two paired wins, six losses and eight ties; its descriptive four-cluster percentile interval was -50 to 0 percentage points. The primary excludes exact citation closure. Fully traced optimal completion was 0/16, 0/16, 1/16 and 0/16: a severe limit on end-to-end interpretation. In five empty book answers, a post-cohort literal audit finds the correct optimal job set in the reason while the plan is withheld for missing evidence. This does not change the registered scores. The book condition used about 1.64 times the input of ordinary preparation without a measured primary gain. The result concerns submitted planning and deferral in this lossy chain; it does not establish a general reading benefit or impaired reasoning.

Natural voice, self-chosen values, long-run interaction and subjective experience remain separate questions. The methodological contribution links perspective-specific continuity, revision of evaluation criteria, distinct ways of attending to a shared problem, and evidence for each transition from representation to outcome. It is situated alongside truth maintenance, belief revision, event sourcing and current identity-contract research. Local precommitments, cases, software and scoring share developer provenance; independent registration, historical priority and external peer review are not claimed.
